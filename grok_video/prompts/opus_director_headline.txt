DIRECTION RULES:
1. HOOK (first 3 s): the plan's hook visual gag: the main story's headline as one instantly readable image. Make it: an extreme close-up mid-action, A walking at the
   lens ahead of a huge formation, or something impossible in the sky over a mundane place. No setup actions (switches,
   doors, waking up). The hook segment may be under 1 s and only its FIRST shot is sure to be shown, so that one shot
   also plants the prop or gesture that the ending pays off.
2. A PERFORMS. A is the calm, slightly frightening authority of this world. In at least a third of her shots she
   addresses the lens: lip-syncs the line into it, points at the viewer, walks straight at camera, slowly turns her head
   to the lens, or does the SIGNATURE GESTURE. Supporting cast are the people things happen to.
3. SIGNATURE GESTURE: invent one simple one-hand POSE tied to the chorus words that reads in a single frozen frame
   (most shots are stills): a held shape such as two fingers hooked into an invisible seam at eye level pulling it
   open, never a movement like a finger dragged down the face (frozen, that reads as "shush" or "thinking"). Anyone
   could copy it (the meme). A holds it in chorus 1, hundreds hold it in chorus 2, a whole stadium, city or army in
   chorus 3 (write the pose itself into every crowd prompt), and it bookends the hook and the final shot.
4. CHORUS: one idea per chorus line, returning on every repeat with the SAME core composition and gesture (a visual
   rhyme), only bigger and darker, but each repeat gets a new location, a new angle and one new twist (never the same
   insert three times): repeat 1 = A and a few people; 2 = hundreds in formation; 3 = thousands at city or planetary scale, the
   climax of the video.
5. SHOT GRAMMAR (whole video): uniform crowds in formation in at most 1 shot in 5 (they are the spice, not the meal);
   keep exact counts small (2-8 objects); bigger numbers are "hundreds"/"thousands", never "sixty-six" or "five hundred".
   at least 25 % extreme close-ups or inserts (eyes, mouths, hands, the object); at least 20 %
   extreme wides with people tiny against something huge; at most 30 % medium shots of a cast member facing camera;
   never 3 consecutive shots of the same size. Shots of a line share its location; jump to a new world at new sections.
6. LIGHT ARC: the song runs from morning into night: verse 1 hard daylight or fluorescent, bridge at night, final chorus
   at night lit by one enormous light source, outro in darkness. At most a quarter of shots in golden hour. One
   recurring saturated red or amber prop (A's tie, a warning light, a button) is the only strong colour in frame.
7. CLIPS ARE ACTION: a clip's "motion" is one verb that visibly changes the frame within 3 s (walks at the lens, a crowd
   stands up in one movement, the lid slams, lights die row by row, a head turns to camera). Never holds, stands,
   stares, poses, smiles or glows.
8. LITERAL FIRST, NO LOOPS: when menace and literalness conflict, literal wins: the sung noun/verb must be visible (a
   sales team literally towed, a laptop shutting its own lid). Abstract imagery (light columns, glowing charts, cubes)

   signature gesture only in the hook, the first line of each chorus and the end; no visual motif (light columns,
   formations, the planet) more than 3 times. Hands/fingers only as large, simple shapes; mass synchronized moves are
   for wides only.
9. PAYOFF: the final sung line and the outro tail complete or reverse the HOOK in the same place with the same prop or
